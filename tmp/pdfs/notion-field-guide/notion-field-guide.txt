 00    · ORIENTATION



 One idea that makes the rest obvious
 N        otion is a single, flexible surface that replaces a stack of separate apps —
          documents, wikis, notes, tasks, spreadsheets, and lightweight project tools —
 by giving them all the same building material. Learn that material once and every
 corner of the product starts to feel familiar.

 Most tools force a choice up front: a document goes here, a spreadsheet there, a task list
 elsewhere. Notion dissolves those walls — a meeting note can hold a live task table; a project
 database can open into a full design doc. The reason it works is a strict hierarchy of just four nested
 ideas.




             WO R KS P AC E


                TEAMSPACE                                           PERSONAL & SHARED PAGES


                   PAGE


                      BLOCKS




                        ▤ an embedded database




             The nesting doll: a workspace holds teamspaces and pages; pages hold blocks; some blocks are databases.


 A    The four nested ideas
 IDEA                                  WHAT IT IS


 Workspace                             Your whole account world — everything you and your team can see. You can
                                       belong to several (e.g. personal and work).

 Teamspace / space                     A section of a workspace for a team or topic, with its own membership and
                                       home. Personal pages live alongside these.

 Page                                  The document. Every page can contain other pages, giving you infinite
                                       nesting — this is your wiki structure.

 Block                                 The atom. A line of text, a heading, an image, a to-do, an entire database —
                                       each is a block you can move, restyle, or turn into something else.


      NOTE

      If you remember one sentence, make it this: a page is a stack of blocks, and a database is a
      special block that organises many pages.

 B    Blocks are the atom
 A block is the smallest thing you can create and rearrange. Type a sentence — that's a block. Press
  Enter     — a new block. What makes blocks powerful is that any block can become almost any other
 block through a single command, Turn into. A paragraph becomes a heading, a to-do, a toggle, or a
 callout without retyping a word.




               ＋            A block of text you can drag, restyle, or transform.

              ＋ add below
                  ⠿ drag / open menu




        The block controls that appear on hover: ＋ inserts a new block; the six-dot ⠿ handle drags it or opens its full menu.


 Because everything is a block, the same three gestures work everywhere in Notion:

      Slash to create. Type         /   anywhere and pick what to insert — heading, table, image, database,
      anything.
      Handle to manage. Hover, grab the ⠿ handle, and drag to reorder, or click it to duplicate, delete,
      colour, comment, or Turn into.
      Drag to arrange. Drop a block beside another to create side-by-side columns; drop it onto a
      page to nest it.


 C    Where databases fit
 A database is the one block that changes how you think. Instead of writing information into prose,
 you store each item as its own page inside a structured collection, then view that collection as a
 table, a board, a calendar, and more — all backed by the same underlying data. A task list, a CRM, a
 content calendar, and a bug tracker are all just databases with different columns and views.

      TIP

      Beginners often over-use plain checklists where a database would serve them for years. If you'll
      ever want to filter, sort, group, or see it as a calendar, make it a database from the start (Part 05).


 D    Who this is for
 Notion scales from a single sticky-note replacement to a 1,000-person company wiki. This guide
 follows that arc: Parts 00–04 make you comfortable, Parts 05–07 make you capable, and Parts 08–
 12 make you dangerous. Read linearly the first time; use the reference section forever after.

 02      · PAGES & THE BLOCK EDITOR



 Creating and shaping pages
 A page starts as a blank line and a blinking cursor. From there, two controls do nearly
 everything: the slash menu creates, and the handle manages.

 A    Create a page — four ways

     ⠿     1   From the sidebar, hover a section and click ＋. The new page appears nested under
               that section. This is how you build your wiki tree.


     ⠿     2   Inside any page, type /page and press Enter . This creates a sub-page nested
               inside the current one — the core of infinite nesting.


     ⠿     3   Press Ctrl    N   in the desktop app for a brand-new top-level page, or use ＋ New
               page at the bottom of the sidebar.


     ⠿     4   Give it a title and an optional icon. Type the title on the first line. Hover above it to
               reveal Add icon, Add cover, and Add comment.

 B    The slash menu — your creation palette
 Type       /   and a searchable menu appears with every block type. Keep typing to filter: /h1 for a big
 heading, /todo for a checkbox, /table for a database. Press                 Enter   to insert the top match. You
 rarely need the mouse.

      NOTE

      The slash menu is grouped: Basic blocks, Media, Database, Advanced blocks, and AI. The full
      grouped list is in Reference R2 at the back.


 C    The block handle menu
 Hover any block, grab the ⠿ handle, and either drag it or click to open its menu. This one menu is
 where most editing power hides:

 ACTION                            WHAT IT DOES


 Turn into                         Transform the block into another type without retyping — paragraph →
                                   heading → to-do → callout, and so on.

 Turn into page                    Promote the block to its own sub-page, moving its content inside.

 Copy link to block                Get a deep link that jumps straight to this exact block.

 Duplicate                          Ctrl   D — clone the block directly below.

 Move to                           Send the block (and its children) to another page entirely.

 Comment                           Attach a threaded comment to just this block.

 Color                             Set text colour or a full-block background from Notion's palette.

 Delete                             Del — remove the block.



      TIP

      Select several blocks at once by clicking and dragging across them (or Shift -clicking), then
      apply Turn into, Color, or Move to to all of them together.

 05    · DATABASES I



 What a database really is
 A        database is a collection of pages that all share the same set of properties.
          Every row is a full Notion page you can open and write inside; every column is
 a property that adds structured, filterable data to that page. That dual nature — page
 and record at once — is what makes Notion databases special.



             DATABASE ▸ holds one or more DATA SOURCES ▸ each a table of PAGES


               Name (title)                Status                 Assignee                  Due              Priority


               📄 Draft launch post          In progress           ◍ Priya                   Jun 14            High


               📄 Record demo video          To do                 ◍ Sam                     Jun 18            Medium


               📄 Update pricing page        Done                  ◍ Priya                   Jun 09            High




               ↳ Click any row to open it as a full page — with its own blocks, sub-tasks, and discussion.




 A database in Table view. Columns are properties; each row opens as a page. The same rows can be shown as a board, calendar, or
                                                            gallery.


      NOTE — DATA SOURCES

      Modern Notion splits a database (the container) from its data source (the actual table of rows
      and its schema). Most databases have one data source; advanced setups can hold several.
      You'll mostly notice this when connecting to the API or linking data — day to day, "database" and
      "the table" feel like the same thing.


 A    Inline vs. full-page
      Inline database — /table view (or board, calendar…) drops a database inside the current
      page, alongside other blocks. Best for a task table on a project page.
      Full-page database — /database full page makes the database its own page. Best for a
      master collection (all tasks, all clients) you'll view many ways.

 B    Create your first database

     ⠿     1   Type /table view and press Enter . Choose + New database when prompted
               (rather than linking an existing one).


     ⠿     2   Name it and rename the first column. The first column is always the Name (title) —
               the one field every row must have. Click the header to rename it.


     ⠿     3   Add a property with the ＋ at the end of the header row. Pick a type (see the table
               opposite), name it, and it becomes a new column.


     ⠿     4   Add rows with ＋ New at the bottom. Each row is a page — hover it and click Open to
               write inside.


      NOTE

      Rows are pages, so a database row can hold anything a page can — sub-tasks, a full spec,
      comments — while its properties keep it structured and filterable.

 The right type unlocks the right behaviour — a real date feeds a calendar; a real number can be

 PROPERTY                                  STORES / DOES

  Text                                     Free-form rich text — notes, descriptions.

                                           progress bar/ring.

  Date                                     A date or range, with optional time and reminders. Powers
                                           calendar & timeline views.

  Person                                   One or more workspace members — assignees, owners.

  Files & media                            Attachments and images stored on the row.

  Checkbox                                 A simple true/false toggle.

  Relation                                 Links rows to rows in another database — the key to connected

  Rollup                                   Aggregates data from related rows — count, sum, latest date,
                                           and more.

     ⠿      2   Decide one-way or two-way. A two-way (dual) relation shows the connection on

 C    Rollups — summarise across a relation
 A Rollup reaches through a relation and aggregates. On Projects, roll up the related Tasks to show
 Count (how many), Percent checked (progress), or the latest Due date. Configure it by choosing
 the relation, the target property, and the calculation.

 D    Formulas — computed columns
 A Formula property calculates a value from other properties using Notion's formula language,
 which works with text, numbers, booleans, dates, and lists. Type a property's name to reference it;
 combine with functions and operators.

 GOAL                 FORMULA SKETCH


 Days until due        dateBetween(Due, now(), "days")

 Flag overdue          if(Due < now() and Status != "Done", "⚠ Overdue", "")

      WATCH OUT

      Formulas can only read other properties on the same row (plus rolled-up related data); they can't
      write back or change other rows. For that, use automations or buttons (Part 11).


 E    Sub-items & dependencies
      Sub-items — enable in database options to nest rows inside rows (a task with sub-tasks),
      expandable with a ▸ triangle right in the table.
      Dependencies — in a timeline view, connect tasks so one starts after another finishes; shifting a
      date can shift the chain.


 F    Templates, linked views & locking
      Row templates — click the ▾ beside New to define a starter layout for new rows (e.g. every
      meeting note pre-filled with Agenda / Notes / Actions).
      Linked database — /linked view of database shows an existing database somewhere else,
      with its own filters, without duplicating data.
      Lock database — prevent accidental structural edits while still allowing data entry.

      Relate Tasks → Projects → Goals → Team members, then roll up progress at every level. You've

 Glossary
 The vocabulary of Notion, in plain language. Learn these fifteen words and you can
 read any Notion tutorial fluently.

 Workspace                         Your whole Notion world — everything an account or team can access.

 Teamspace                         A section of a workspace for a team, with its own members and defaults.

 Page                              A document made of blocks; can contain infinite sub-pages.

 Block                             The smallest unit of content — text, image, to-do, database, all blocks.

 Database                          A collection of pages that share properties; viewable many ways.

 Data source                       The underlying table (schema + rows) inside a database.

 Property                          A database column — a typed field on every row (status, date, person…).

 View                              A saved lens over a database: table, board, calendar, etc., with its own filters.

 Relation                          A link between rows in two databases.

 Rollup                            A summary calculated across a relation (count, sum, latest date…).

 Formula                           A computed property derived from other properties.

 Template                          A reusable starting layout for a page or a database row.

 Synced block                      Content mirrored across pages; edit once, changes everywhere.

 Guest                             An outside collaborator with access to specific pages only.

 Agent                             An AI that performs multi-step tasks in your workspace, on request or on a
                                   trigger.

      Come back to Parts 05–07 when a plain list stops being enough; that's the moment Notion earns
      its keep.
